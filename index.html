Why This Ward, Why Now

A ward being built in real time — and getting it wrong

The story of northwest London is the story of growth that outran its own infrastructure. Subdivisions on Sunningdale were approved and sold for a decade before the road was widened. There are still no bus routes in large parts of the ward. A new school and a new library — both needed — are years behind.

That's not inevitable. It's a planning and prioritization failure. And the councillor who represents Ward 7 for the next four years will sit on the Planning and Environment Committee, the Infrastructure and Corporate Services Committee, and the Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee — the exact bodies that make those decisions.

I'm running because the north BRT application is live right now. The Sunningdale build-out is ongoing. The development charges policy is being reviewed. The decisions made in 2026–2030 will determine whether Ward 7 becomes a connected, complete community — or another car-dependent suburb that residents regret in twenty years.

The Issues That Matter Here

What Ward 7 Residents Are Dealing With — and Where I Stand

These aren't hypothetical concerns. They're documented in the city's own budget, planning records, and construction schedules.

Issue

A Ward Being Built Without Its Transit

Sunningdale, Fox Hollow, and Hyde Park have seen thousands of homes built over the past decade. There is still no bus service covering large parts of these subdivisions. The former Ward 7 councillor publicly said "a lot of those subdivisions have been built out for almost a decade and there is no transit — this is a real concern." It remains a real concern today.

Source: CBC London, Aug. 2024 — Ward 7 Coun. Corrine Rahman

My Position

Back the North BRT Application — Loudly and Publicly

The City of London is currently seeking $100 million from the province to build a Bus Rapid Transit route up Western Road and Wharncliffe, terminating at the Masonville transit hub. This route runs through Ward 7. Council killed a north BRT route in 2019. I will be its champion in the 2026–2030 term — because a ward councillor who puts their credibility behind this application changes its political calculus at city hall.

Issue

Growth That Outran Its Own Infrastructure

Sunningdale Road was a two-lane road with a gravel shoulder until 2026. It took over a decade of subdivision approvals — and an $18M provincial grant — to finally fund the widening that's now underway. The same pattern is repeating: 1,360 more units are proposed in the Corlon subdivision at Sunningdale and Wonderland. The Development Charges policy isn't capturing infrastructure costs fast enough.

Source: City of London / Province of Ontario — Sunningdale Road West Improvements project, 2026

My Position

Growth Pays for Growth — Not Existing Residents

Development charges are the mechanism that makes new subdivisions fund the infrastructure they require. The city is currently reviewing its DC policy (Budget Case #P-13). I'll push for DC rates that fully capture the cost of roads, transit connections, and community facilities in new areas — so that the Sunningdale pattern doesn't repeat itself in the next wave of northwest development.

Issue

The Northwest Library Is Delayed — Again

The northwest London library branch — the only planned community hub for Ward 7's growth areas — has been deferred to 2027 in the 2026 budget. The project has $500,000 left in its life-to-date budget for design work. A new library in a rapidly growing area isn't a luxury: it's where people access job resources, digital services, and community connection.

Budget Case #P-15: NW Library branch deferred from 2026 to 2027 · Total project: $6.9M

My Position

2027 is the Hard Deadline — I'll Enforce It

The deferral is on record. The $500K for design work is in place. I will attend every relevant budget and planning meeting where the northwest library timeline is a variable, and I will make clear publicly when it is at risk. Ward 7 residents deserve a community hub. I'll make it politically costly to defer this again.

Issue

LMCH Is Under Financial Pressure While Serving 5,000+ Residents

London and Middlesex Community Housing — which houses over 5,000 residents citywide, many of them in vulnerable circumstances — is being squeezed by rising property taxes on the homes it owns. Its 2023 base property tax cost alone was $5.4 million. Without operating support, LMCH has responded by deferring hiring and delaying maintenance. The 2026 budget allocates only $641,000 in additional support — far less than the gap.

Budget Case #P-1: $641K additional LMCH operating support approved for 2026

My Position

Stable LMCH Funding is the Most Direct Lever on Housing

A city councillor can't build housing. But they can fight to ensure the organization that already houses 5,000 low-income Londoners doesn't deteriorate. I'll advocate for LMCH funding that keeps pace with actual cost pressures — not a token allocation that defers the reckoning to next year's budget. Deferred maintenance in social housing always costs more in the long run.

Issue

Climate Fund Eliminated While Grant Leverage Disappears

The 2026 budget eliminates the Climate Change Reserve Fund contribution entirely — saving $384,000 while forfeiting grant leverage. Urban Forestry has been invited to submit a full application to the Federation of Canadian Municipalities' Green Municipal Fund, but city funding is required to match 50% of costs. Without the municipal contribution, London can't access the matching dollars.

Budget Case #P-24: Climate Reserve Fund eliminated — FCM Green Fund matching at risk

My Position

Restore the Climate Fund — The Grant Math is Obvious

Eliminating a $384,000 annual contribution that unlocks matching federal grants is not fiscal prudence — it's penny-wise and pound-foolish. I'll move a motion to restore the Climate Change Reserve Fund contribution and require staff to report on the FCM grant application status each budget cycle. London's tree canopy is an equity issue too: wealthier wards have more trees.

Full Platform

Five Commitments for Ward 7's Next Four Years

Each one is anchored to a real decision this council will make — not a vague aspiration, but a named vote, a named committee, a named outcome.

01

Win the North BRT for Ward 7

The city is asking the province for $100 million to build rapid transit up Western Road and Wharncliffe, terminating at Masonville — the busiest transit node in the north end. That application is live now. The 2026–2030 council will govern through the funding decision and approval process. I will champion this route publicly, loudly, and at every relevant council and SPPC meeting — because the ward councillor who represents the Masonville corridor should be its most vocal advocate, not a passive observer. In 2019 a different council killed this route when it had committed funding. That mistake should not be repeated.

Transit
02

Make Development Charges Work the Way They're Supposed To

Development charges are how new growth is supposed to pay for the infrastructure it requires. The Sunningdale pattern — subdivisions approved and filled for a decade before Sunningdale Road got sidewalks — is a DC policy failure. The DC bylaw is under review as part of the 2026 budget cycle (Budget Case #P-13). I will push for DC rates that fully reflect the cost of road-widening, transit connections, school sites, and parkland in new growth areas — so Ward 7 residents aren't paying for development after the fact.

Fiscal
03

Northwest Library Branch — 2027, Not 2028

The northwest library branch serving Ward 7's growth areas was deferred to 2027 in the 2026 budget. Design funding of $500,000 is in place. That is not a guarantee; budgets get amended. I will track every budget cycle and every relevant capital report until ground is broken. Libraries are not luxuries in a growth community — they are where people access employment services, digital infrastructure, and community space. I will make it publicly uncomfortable to defer this project again.

Community
04

Complete Streets in Ward 7's New Neighbourhoods

The Local Road Reconstruction Program was cut by $114,000 in 2026 (Budget Case #P-6), removing roughly one street per year from the repair schedule. Over 600 streets citywide are in very poor condition. I will monitor the capital plan to ensure Ward 7's older residential streets — the established areas of Medway and Stoneybrook that predate the current growth wave — receive their fair share. I'll also push for every new subdivision approval in Ward 7 to include concrete sidewalk, cycling, and transit stub requirements, not as afterthoughts but as conditions of approval.

Infrastructure
05

Planning That Actually Leads Growth

Ward 7 is where London's growth is happening. The Planning and Environment Committee — which I will seek to sit on — reviews every major development application, official plan amendment, and area plan in the city. For Ward 7 applications specifically, I will push for conditions that require traffic studies, transit plans, and community facility timelines before approvals, not after. The Corlon proposal alone adds 1,360 units at Sunningdale and Wonderland. That decision will shape the north end for a generation. It deserves a ward councillor who reads the full application, understands the infrastructure gaps, and votes accordingly.
